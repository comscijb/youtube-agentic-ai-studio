# Working title
He Woke Up and Everyone in the World Was Him

# Narrative type
B

# Logline
A man wakes up in a world where every person has his face, his voice and his habits, and finally learns what it is like to live with him.

# Protagonist
Daniel Moss, 34, sales manager. Short dark hair, light stubble, navy quarter-zip sweater, a phone always in his hand. Charming, quick, talks over people, tells small lies, turns every story to himself. Wound: the overlooked middle child who learned to fill every silence to be noticed. Inner belief: "I'm easy to be around."

# Other characters
- Claire Moss, 33, his wife, a nurse. Long light-brown hair, green scrubs or a grey sweater. Patient, tired, rarely gets to finish a sentence.
- Everyone else: during the day of the rule, every other person is Daniel, same face and voice, in their own clothes (a barista's apron, a boss's suit) so roles stay readable.

# Setting
Present day: the Moss apartment (bedroom, small kitchen), Daniel's open-plan office, a busy coffee shop. The rule: for one day, every other person in the world is Daniel, and nobody but him notices anything strange. It is never explained.

# Beats
- Cold open: the kitchen at night. Across the table, his wife, with his own face and voice, glances at her phone and says his favourite line back to him: "I'm just being honest." "It was the first time Daniel heard what he sounded like." Cut to the morning.
- Hook: Tuesday morning. Daniel wakes, and the person beside him in bed turns over. It is him. The neighbour, the barista: every face is his. To them, nothing has changed.
- Strategy works: at first it is wonderful. Everyone laughs at his jokes. His boss, Daniel, approves his pitch in thirty seconds. For one afternoon the world is perfectly easy.
- Hidden cost: by evening the Daniels are unbearable. They interrupt, check their phones mid-sentence, arrive late with a charming excuse, turn every story back to themselves. A barista-Daniel calls him "buddy" and gets his order wrong without listening. Daniel recognises every move, and every one of them is his.
- Reversal: at dinner, Daniel admits something real: he is scared he will lose his job. His wife-Daniel glances at her phone and says, "You'll be fine, you always are." It is exactly what he says to Claire whenever she tells him something hard. He understands that Claire has lived in this world every day: a world with one Daniel in it was already this.
- Philosophical layer: never explained. We never meet ourselves from the outside; maybe empathy means standing on the other side of our own habits.
- Ending: the next morning Claire is Claire again. She starts telling him about a dream. Daniel feels the reflex to reach for his phone, to make a joke, to say "you'll be fine". He doesn't. He listens to the end. She stops, puzzled: "You're quiet. Are you okay?" She has never seen him just listen. Final narrated line: "Everyone you know has spent a day in a world with you in it. What was it like for them?"

# Tone
Darkly funny at first, then uncomfortable and intimate. No horror, no villain: Daniel is likeable, which is the point.

# Must include
- the cold-open line "I'm just being honest" in his own voice
- every face being Daniel's while nobody else notices
- his habits shown, not listed: interrupting, the phone, small lies, lateness
- "You'll be fine, you always are" as the reversal
- the final morning where he simply listens

# Must avoid
- explaining the phenomenon (science, magic, simulation)
- body horror or a horror tone
- a lecture about empathy or self-improvement
- crowds; at most three or four Daniels in one image
- Claire as villain or saint; a neat promise to change

# Target minutes
11

# Title ideas
- He Woke Up and Everyone in the World Was Him
- For One Day, Everyone Became Him

# Thumbnail idea
A coffee shop where the barista and two customers all have Daniel's face, turned toward the real Daniel, who stares back in quiet shock. Text: "Everyone is me."
