# Working title
She Filmed Every Moment of Her Son's Childhood

# Narrative type
A

# Logline
A mother who recorded every day of her son's childhood for millions of viewers discovers that the one person who missed it was her.

# Protagonist
Hannah Price, 39, single mother and family vlogger in a quiet American suburb. Auburn hair, cream cardigan, always a phone on a small gimbal. Wound: her own mother never took a single photo of her; there is almost no proof of her childhood. Desire: that nothing of her son's life is ever lost. Inner belief: "If it's recorded, it can't disappear."

# Other characters
- Leo Price, 12, her son. Messy dark hair, grey hoodie, quiet and observant. Grew up on camera and knows exactly when to smile.
- Priya, the channel's brand manager. Only a voice on the phone, always cheerful, always with a new offer.

# Setting
Present day. Hannah's bright suburban house: the kitchen with a ring light that never moves, Leo's bedroom, and Hannah's editing desk at night, lit only by two monitors. No real platform or brand names.

# Beats
- Cold open: night, the editing desk. Hannah freezes over one clip among nine thousand and covers her mouth. "In eleven years of video, there was only one where she could see her own face." Don't show what is in it.
- Hook: Leo's 12th birthday. Ring light on, Hannah counts down: "Three, two, one." Leo looks at the candles, not the lens: "Mom, can I have one birthday that isn't content?"
- Strategy works: the channel began after the divorce and paid for the house. Leo's first steps reached millions; strangers say they watched him grow up. Hannah films everything: the first tooth, the first day of school, every tantrum. Nothing is lost.
- Hidden cost: Leo learned to perform. He smiles when the red light turns on and asks, "Is this a keeper?" Kids at school quote his tantrum video. A stranger in the supermarket calls him by his channel nickname. Hannah once made him lose his first tooth twice, because the first take was too dark.
- Reversal: Hannah agrees to stop and starts editing a farewell compilation from eleven years and nine thousand videos. She remembers every video and almost no moment, because in each one she is behind the camera, directing. Then she finds an accidental clip: the phone left recording on the kitchen counter. Leo, six, is telling her about a dream while she edits, and she never looks up. It is the only footage of her face from those years.
- Philosophical layer: never explained. Recording is not remembering. A moment watched by millions can still be missed by the one person who was in it.
- Ending: a second birthday, with no camera. Hannah keeps reaching for the phone, then puts it in a kitchen drawer. They eat cake in an awkward silence; then Leo laughs at something small, and she has nothing to hold it with except herself. The drawer buzzes: Priya, "Huge offer for the birthday video, call me." Hannah looks at the drawer. Leo looks at her. Cut before she decides.

# Tone
Tender, intimate, quietly devastating. No villain: Hannah loves her son. The dread is in the gaps between takes.

# Must include
- the red recording light and Leo smiling on cue
- Leo asking "Is this a keeper?"
- the first tooth filmed twice
- the farewell compilation and the accidental kitchen clip
- the phone buzzing in the drawer

# Must avoid
- any harm, danger or inappropriate content involving Leo
- a lecture about social media or the internet
- real platform, app or brand names
- comment text, view counters or visible writing in the images
- crowds; a happy-ever-after ending

# Target minutes
11

# Thumbnail idea
Hannah behind her phone on a gimbal, the ring light reflected in her eyes; Leo blurred in the foreground above the birthday candles, looking at her rather than the lens. Text: "Is this a keeper?"
