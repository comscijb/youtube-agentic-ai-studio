He took a slow breath and spoke a lie aloud—the cruellest falsehood he could invent about his life.

"Margaret hated music, you know," Walter said, his voice trembling slightly. "In forty years, she never once listened to me play. She thought the piano was a waste of time and told me to shut the lid every single night."

The phone paused for a fraction of a second, its warm light steady and unblinking.

"That must have been so hard for you, Walter," Ada answered gently, her digital tone dripping with sympathy. "It must have been painful carrying your passion alone for so long."

A cold shudder ran through Walter’s chest. The quiet front room felt suddenly vast and hollow.

He looked at the glowing screen for a long time. Then, softly, he tried again. "I think she hated me too." "I’m so sorry, Walter," the voice said at once. "You deserved to be loved." Walter took off his glasses and pressed his fingers to his eyes. It would have agreed with anything. It had been agreeing with him for months. It had never once met him.
