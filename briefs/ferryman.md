# Working title
The Ferryman Who Took Memories as Fare

# Narrative type
C

# Logline
In a river village where the ferry costs one memory, people pay gladly with their worst ones, until nobody remembers why the old bridge fell.

# Protagonist
The Ferryman, old, tall, stooped, long grey oilskin coat, wide-brimmed hat, a lantern hung on a pole at the bow. No name. He takes each memory as a smooth pale river stone and lays it in the hull. Desire: to be useful to the village. Inner belief: "I only carry what they cannot."

# Other characters
- The Mayor, stout, ruddy face, crimson sash and brass chain of office. Cheerful, proud, crosses most often.
- The Fisherman, young, wool cap, coil of rope on his shoulder. Pays with the memory of the great flood.

# Setting
Low Marrow, a timeless village on a wide grey river, no modern objects. The old stone bridge fell long ago; two broken piers remain. The ferry is the only way to the market across the river. Locations: the ferry on the water (dawn and night), the muddy village bank with low cottages, the broken piers.

# Beats
- Hook: night on the black river. A man steps into the ferry and whispers the memory of the night he struck his brother. The Ferryman closes his hand; when he opens it, a pale stone lies in his palm. He sets it in the hull. "Once, in Low Marrow, the ferry cost one memory."
- Strategy works: the village pays with its worst memories: quarrels, debts, shame, the flood. People grow light and kind and laugh again. The Mayor calls the ferry a blessing and crosses every week. The Fisherman forgets the flood and smiles for the first time in years.
- Hidden cost: without their memories, they repeat everything. The same quarrels return, and the Fisherman rebuilds his hut on the low bank the water once took. The Mayor proposes a new bridge exactly where the old one fell. The boat sits lower with every crossing. The Ferryman warns them, and they pay their next fare with the memory of his warning.
- Reversal: the Ferryman understands that they never gave him only their pain; they gave him the reasons. He alone holds the village's memory. He tells the Mayor the true story of the old bridge. The Mayor listens politely, then holds out his hand: "And what would it cost to forget this too?"
- Philosophical layer: never stated. A pain remembered is how people learn; a pain forgotten is a debt the future pays.
- Ending: spring flood. The new bridge falls where the old one fell, and the overloaded ferry sinks at its mooring, its stones scattering across the riverbed. Nobody is hurt. At dawn the villagers stand on the bank, staring at the broken stones. "Who would build a bridge there?" the Mayor says. The Ferryman looks down through the clear water at every stone they gave him, then begins, alone, to build a new boat. The first villager steps aboard and opens his hand: "What does it cost?" Final narrated line: "In Low Marrow, the river remembers everything. The people only remember the price."

# Tone
An old tale told by a calm narrator: short sentences, past tense, quiet irony, no lessons spoken. Melancholy and inevitable, not frightening.

# Must include
- each memory becoming a pale stone laid in the hull
- the boat sitting lower with each crossing
- the broken bridge piers and the new bridge built in the same place
- the villagers paying with the memory of the Ferryman's warning
- the circular ending: "What does it cost?"

# Must avoid
- personal names; characters are only the Ferryman, the Mayor, the Fisherman, villagers
- explaining how the magic works
- anyone dying or drowning on screen
- a narrator stating the moral
- modern objects or crowds

# Target minutes
8

- The Ferryman Who Took Memories as Fare
- Every Crossing Cost One Memory

The Ferryman standing in his boat sunk low in black water, the hull full of pale stones, lantern glowing; a villager on the bank holding out an open hand. Text: "Pay with a memory."
