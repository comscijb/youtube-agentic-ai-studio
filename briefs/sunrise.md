# Working title
The Man Who Bought the Sunrise

# Narrative type
C

# Logline
A merchant buys the sunrise and charges his village to look at it, until the village learns to turn west and he is left owning every dawn alone.

# Protagonist
The Merchant, sixties, tall and gaunt, dark fur-trimmed coat, a heavy gold signet ring he presses into wax. No name. The richest man in Eastmere. Inner belief: "What is free is never valued." Desire: to own the one thing everyone loves.

# Other characters
- The Daughter, young woman, plain grey dress, long dark braid. Loves him, and loves the dawn more.
- Villagers: never named, a few figures at a time.

# Setting
Eastmere, a timeless valley village. Every dawn the village climbs the eastern ridge to watch the sun rise together. Locations: the eastern ridge (later the Merchant's wall, gate, bell and tower), the village square, the western hill at dusk.

# Beats
- Cold open: omen in the narrator's voice over a vast golden dawn and one small figure on a tower: "This is the story of the man who owned the morning, and of the last sunrise he ever shared."
- Hook: the old lord is ruined, and the Merchant buys his last possession: a deed, sealed in red wax with the gold ring, granting "the eastern sky, the sunrise, and the right to all who look upon it". At dawn he stands on the ridge and announces: "From tomorrow, the morning has a price."
- Strategy works: he builds a wall along the ridge with one gate and one bell. Each dawn the villagers pay a coin to pass. The coins pile up; he builds a tower and watches the sun rise alone from the top, above everyone. The Daughter begs him to open the gate. He refuses: "What is free is never valued."
- Hidden cost: the villagers stop paying. One evening they climb the western hill instead and watch the sun go down together, with lanterns, music, bread. Soon dusk is where the village lives: weddings, songs, laughter. Furious, he raises the wall until even the valley lies in shadow each morning. The Daughter marries at sunset on the western hill. She asks him to come. He stays on his tower, the sun rising behind him over an empty valley.
- Reversal: one winter dawn he unbars the gate himself and rings the bell, again and again, for the whole village. Nobody comes. They have learned to sleep through mornings and to love the evening without him. Holding the deed, he understands that he bought the sunrise, but never the people he once watched it with.
- Philosophical layer: never stated. What we own we hold alone; what we share is the only thing that stays.
- Ending: years later the wall is cracked, the gate rusted open, the bell silent. Every dawn an old man climbs his tower with the deed in his coat. Every dusk, far to the west, lanterns rise and laughter carries across the valley. He never walks there. Final narrated line: "He owned every sunrise for the rest of his life. He never shared another."

# Tone
An old tale told by a calm, grave narrator: short sentences, past tense, mounting tragedy, cold irony. Dramatic contrasts: the blazing gold dawn against his solitude, the warm copper dusk against his wall.

# Must include
- the deed sealed in red wax with the gold ring
- the wall, the gate, the bell and the coin at dawn
- the village turning west to watch the sunset
- the Daughter's sunset wedding he does not attend
- the dawn he rings the bell and nobody comes

# Must avoid
- personal names; only the Merchant, the Daughter, villagers
- magic or explanation: the absurdity is only the deed
- anyone dying, illness, violence
- a narrator stating the moral; the Merchant as a cartoon villain
- modern objects; crowds (a few villagers per image)

# Target minutes
8

# Title ideas
- The Man Who Bought the Sunrise
- He Owned Every Sunrise and Shared None

# Thumbnail idea
A tiny figure on top of a stone tower against an enormous blazing gold sun, his long shadow stretching over an empty valley; a wall below with a closed gate. Text: "He owned the sunrise."
